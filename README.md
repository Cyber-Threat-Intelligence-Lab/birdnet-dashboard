# BirdNET Biodiversity Dashboard

The **BirdNET Biodiversity Dashboard** is a UNSW Sydney research project for
monitoring and exploring biodiversity through passive acoustic sensing.

The project brings together expertise from the **School of Biological, Earth &
Environmental Sciences (BEES)** and the **Cyber Threat Intelligence (CTI) Lab**
in the **School of Computer Science and Engineering (CSE)** at UNSW Sydney.

## About the Project

A network of **Raspberry Pi-based acoustic monitoring devices running BirdNET**
has been deployed around **Smithfield Lake, UNSW**.

These devices continuously monitor the local acoustic environment and use
BirdNET to identify species from their vocalisations and other sounds.

The collected observations are processed and presented through this dashboard,
providing an ongoing view of biodiversity detected in the area.

The dashboard is designed to support the observation and analysis of Australian
biodiversity, with a particular focus on species occurring around the
Smithfield Lake monitoring site.

## Biodiversity Monitoring

The dashboard brings together detections of wildlife including:

- Birds
- Mammals
- Insects
- Other identifiable fauna captured by the acoustic monitoring system

For detected species, the dashboard provides information about the species
along with temporal patterns in their detections.

## Temporal Analysis

Species detections can be explored across multiple time scales, including:

- **Hourly frequency** — how detections vary throughout the day
- **Daily frequency** — changes in detections between days
- **Monthly frequency** — longer-term patterns in species activity

These views help reveal when different species are acoustically active and how
their observed frequency changes over time.

## Data Updates

The dashboard and its underlying detection data are **refreshed every 24 hours**.

This allows newly processed observations from the deployed monitoring devices
to be incorporated into the dashboard on a daily basis.

## Species Information

Species pages combine the observations collected by the monitoring system with
additional information to provide context about detected wildlife.

Species photographs and supporting species information are sourced with thanks
to **iNaturalist** and its community of contributors.


## Australian Biodiversity

Australia has a distinctive and highly diverse fauna, and continuous acoustic
monitoring provides a way to observe parts of that biodiversity with minimal
disturbance to wildlife.

This project explores how edge computing, automated species recognition and
long-term environmental monitoring can be combined to provide an accessible
view of biodiversity around the monitored UNSW site.
